## What Adored is

Adored is where a person records a collection and decides what they love most in it. It is
**pre-marketplace**: the step before anything is listed, sold, or given away. People arrive knowing
resale apps (eBay, Poshmark, Vinted, Vestiaire); Adored should feel like that genre, but cozier,
because here the objects are yours and the decisions are personal.

## Who it's for

One person, sorting their own things. Occasionally they invite someone to see a finished ranking.
They are not a power user and they are not running a shop.

## What it does

1. **Record.** Photograph objects, give each a page with its photos and details (brand, style,
   color, material, category, size, story). Editing should feel like filling in a listing you
   already know how to use.
2. **Decide.** Help the person find what they love most, a little at a time: quick feelings,
   "do you use this?", "which would you reach for first?", then a ranking.
3. **Share.** A finished ranking can be shared by link.

## Principles

- **Familiar first.** Borrow the layouts people already know: item cards in a grid, an item page
  with a photo carousel beside the details, filters, an overflow menu for rarer actions. Don't
  invent a new interaction where a known one works.
- **Affirmation over subtraction.** The headline is what you love; letting go is a quiet
  consequence. Prefer "which would you reach for first?" over "keep or toss?"
- **Low load, not low richness.** One decision in focus at a time, but the surface can be warm,
  colorful, and full of photographs. Grids and lists are fine; they are how people browse their
  own things.
- **Gentle.** Unhurried, reversible, no shaming ("too many"), no streaks or confetti. Undo is
  always near.
- **Photos are the hero.** Big, respectful imagery everywhere an object appears.
- **Tablet-first, phone-ready.** Taps are primary; swipe is fine where it's native (carousels,
  dismiss). Touch targets 44px+. WCAG 2.1 AA; honor reduced motion.

## Not (for now)

- No prices, carts, offers, or selling flows. Pre-marketplace means the hand-off to a marketplace
  happens elsewhere.
- No gamification and no counting against the person.
